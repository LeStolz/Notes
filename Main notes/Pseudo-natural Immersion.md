# Realism
Replicate the real world with high-quality visuals, spatialized sound, realistic physics, and natural interactions, fully stimulating the senses. But technology and behavior replication are often imperfect, so VR is rarely fully realistic. And because of Uncanny Valley effect, VR is often cartoonish.

High realism focuses on enhancing specific aspects to closely replicate real-world experiences, useful for simulators, prototyping, professional training, or games. Challenges include technological and software limitations, and high costs, while benefits include realism, usability, and engagement.

Beyond realism enables new applications by letting users virtually change **time** (past/future, slow/fast), **place** (telepresence, remote collaboration same virtual space, change POV, avatar), and **interactions** (non-natural or impossible in the real world e.g. navigation, physics, ).

**Interaction fidelity** measures how accurately real-world actions are reproduced in an interactive system. High fidelity aims to replicate real movements and controls for training or physical tasks (e.g., swinging a golf club naturally):
- **Magic techniques** lower fidelity to improve usability and give new, unnatural capabilities. **Hypernatural techniques** use natural movements but enhance them for added power or intelligent guidance (e.g., Go-Go technique).
