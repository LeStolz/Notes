# Bad Designs
Techno-centered design is bad because everyone is different:
- Low discoverability (does not provide info on how the system work).
- Assumption that users are all good at tech.
- High training cost.
- High maintenance cost.
- Loss of productivity (user uses better shortcut).
- Incomplete usage (design that users don't notice).
- Annotation (failure of design).
- Loss of credibility.
- Poor [[The H (Human) in HCI|Usability]].
=> User-centered design is better: based on user's needs, computers adapt to facilitate user's life => Must understand user, their task and [[The H (Human) in HCI|human factors]].

> The main reason for resistance to new tech is because of the incompatibility between the product, task, and the user.
# Design Thinking Process
> There must be iteration to progressively refine the product.

Apple's process:
1. [[User Research|Investigate]] > [[User Research Interpretation|Interpret]] (User empathization > Problem space): Discover stakeholders. How is it done now? What is wanted? What has been tried?
2. [[Ideating|Ideate]] (Solution space): Brainstorm, Grasp issues and potential solutions ([[Qualitative Evaluation|guidelines]]).
3. [[Prototyping|Prototype]]: Tangible. Identify challenges, Uncover subtleties
4. [[Prototype Evaluation|Evaluate]]: Discover problems, Assess progress. Determine next step
5. Back to step 1 | 2 | Produce: Build final product. Marketing, support and maintenance
