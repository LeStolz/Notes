#Extended-Reality  #HCI 

> Virtual manipulation involves multimodal sensorimotor interaction for immersion, with haptic perception (kinesthetic forces/movements and tactile skin information) and kinesthetic feedback to apply forces or constrain motion.

Actuators—the key element of kinesthetic feedback—include electric motors (most common), magnetic brakes (for hard impacts), shape memory alloys (for small forces) based on electric current, hydraulic (not used a lot) and pneumatic actuators (natural damping), each converting input (current, pressure) into controlled forces or motions.

# Force feedback devices
![[Haptic Force.png]]
- Active mechatronic systems that transfer forces from a virtual environment and provide reaction forces to the user.
- Require actuators to constrain movement; must operate ≥1 kHz for hard impacts.
- **Portable devices:** Should not restrict user movement; compensate for weight.
- **Non-portable devices:** Design to increase workspace.
- **Other considerations:** Safety (work along power axes), ergonomics (easy to use, adaptable), often based on teleoperation technology.

We can also use haptic feedback to enhance non-haptic information:
- Supplement visual or auditory feedback when they are insufficient.
- Help users interpret multi-attribute data and understand complex phenomena (e.g., shock waves, various flows).
